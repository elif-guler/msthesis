#+TITLE: MGE Permutation Analysis: Summary & Results

* Overview

** Research Question
Are species-specific, pool-unique genomic markers (=FUR-unique markers=) non-randomly clustered near
*Mobile Genetic Elements (MGEs)*—such as transposons, integrases, and phages—or are they randomly
distributed across the reference genome?

** Method
A *Monte Carlo Permutation Test* ($N = 10,000$) implemented via ~gin~. We compare the real *Observed
MGE Overlap Count* against a *Null Distribution ($H_0$)* generated by randomly shuffling marker interval
locations across the reference genome.

* Pipeline Steps

** Step 1: Marker Alignment & Interval Creation (~make_intervals.py~)
- *Input:* Fur marker sequences (=fur_unique.fasta=) and target reference genome (=.fasta=).
- *Logic:* To test spatial proximity, FASTA sequences must be mapped to specific physical coordinates
  (~chr start end~).
- *Mechanism:* Runs ~blastn~ against the reference genome. Only hits meeting strict alignment quality
  thresholds (high % identity and % coverage) are written to ~fur_only_intervals.txt~.

** Step 2: Calculating Observed Overlaps (~annotate_mge.py~)
- *Input:* Reference genome annotation (=.gff=) and placed intervals (~fur_only_intervals.txt~).
- *Logic:* Parses GFF feature attributes (~product=...~) for MGE-related keywords (e.g., /transposase/,
  /integrase/, /phage/, /insertion element/).
- *Output:* Counts how many real marker intervals directly overlap or fall within MGE regions. This single
  integer is the *Observed Overlap Count*.

** Step 3: Generating the Null Distribution (~shuffle~ + ~annotate_mge.py~)
- *Input:* Placed intervals, reference GFF, and $N = 10,000$ permutations.
- *Logic:* Determines expected overlap counts if markers were placed completely at random across the genome.
- *Mechanism:*
  1. ~shuffle~ takes original interval lengths and randomly relocates them across the genome $10,000$ times,
     preserving interval lengths and overall genome size.
  2. For each shuffle, ~annotate_mge.py --stream~ counts MGE overlaps.
- *Output:* ~shuffle_counts.txt~ containing 10,000 integers (the $H_0$ distribution).

** Step 4: Statistical Evaluation & Empirical $p$-Value
- *Logic:* One-sided test measuring how often random chance produces an overlap count $\ge \text{Observed}$.
- *Formula:*
  $$p = \frac{\text{GE} + 1}{N + 1}$$
  - $\text{GE}$: Number of permutations where $\text{Shuffled Overlaps} \ge \text{Observed}$.
  - $+1$: Standard continuity correction to prevent $p = 0$ and bound results at $p > 0$.
  - $N$: Total permutations ($10,000$).

* Edge Cases

1. *Pool Size Trade-Off (100 vs. 1000):*
   - *Pool 100:* Provides an adequate candidate pool of unique markers (e.g., 225 in =mtb=, 202 in =clo=),
     offering statistical power for spatial testing.
   - *Pool 1000:* Expanding the background panel to 1,000 genomes drastically reduces the number of unique
     candidate markers (e.g., 0 in =mtb=, 4 in =clo=) due to extensive pan-genome coverage.

2. *Unmapped Markers in Reference:*
   - For =clo/pool_1000=, 4 unique markers existed, but 0 aligned to the single reference strain under strict
     BLAST criteria. With 0 placed intervals, no permutations can be executed ($p = 1.0$).

3. *Resolution Bounded by Permutations ($p < 1/N$):*
   - With $N = 10,000$, the minimum non-zero empirical $p$-value resolution limit is
     $\frac{1}{10001} \approx 0.0001$.

* Conclusions

1. *No Specific MGE Targeting:*
   - FUR-unique markers do not non-randomly cluster near MGEs. Overlaps occur at rates consistent with
     random chance across the genome ($p > 0.05$ for all datasets).

2. *Panel Size Trade-Off:*
   - Larger background panels ($N = 1000$) eliminate unique candidate markers due to extensive pan-genome
     coverage, leaving pool size 100 as the effective threshold for spatial permutation testing.

3. *Methodological Summary:*
   - The ~gin~ permutation test successfully demonstrated that observed overlaps must always be benchmarked
     against an empirical null distribution to avoid false-positive enrichment claims.

While SeqWin filters out neighbor-absent markers under the assumption that lineage-specific absence is
predominantly driven by Mobile Genetic Elements (MGEs), this analysis demonstrates that FUR-unique markers
are not statistically enriched for MGEs ($p > 0.05$). The observed overlap rates between unique markers and
MGE annotations are fully consistent with a random genomic distribution. Consequently, automatically discarding
neighbor-absent markers on the assumption of MGE involvement unnecessarily restricts the candidate marker pool
without empirical justification.
