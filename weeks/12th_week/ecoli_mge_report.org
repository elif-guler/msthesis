#+TITLE: Evaluation of Mobile Genetic Element (MGE) Enrichment in Clade-Unique Diagnostic Markers Across 6 Escherichia coli Lineages

* Summary

Diagnostic marker selection tools such as *Fur* and *SeqWin* identify sequence regions capable of discriminating specific bacterial phylogroups from neighboring lineages. A critical consideration for diagnostic stability is whether these target markers reside within hypervariable, mobile genomic regions—such as plasmids, prophages, transposons, and insertion sequences (IS elements)—or within stable core genomic loci.

This study evaluated whether unique markers generated by *Fur* for six distinct *Escherichia coli* phylogroups (*A*, *B1*, *B2*, *D*, *E*, and *F*) are significantly enriched within Mobile Genetic Elements (MGEs). Using a Monte Carlo interval permutation test ($N = 10,000$), we constructed empirical null distributions for all clades possessing unique markers (*B2*, *D*, *E*, and *F*). 

**Key Findings**:
1. None of the evaluated *E. coli* clades showed statistically significant enrichment for MGE overlaps ($p > 0.20$).
2. Clades *B2* and *E* exhibited **zero** MGE overlaps ($p = 1.0$), demonstrating that unique markers in these lineages map exclusively to stable genomic regions.
3. Clades *D* and *F* exhibited **2 observed overlaps** each. However, permutation testing revealed that $\ge 2$ overlaps occur naturally by chance in $\approx 20\%$ of random interval placements ($p \approx 0.206$ and $p \approx 0.201$), confirming that these overlaps reflect background genomic MGE density rather than active selection or preferential integration.
4. Clades *A* and *B1* yielded 0 unique FUR markers during initial sequence comparison and were excluded from permutation testing.

* Background

** Diagnostic Stability & Mobile Elements
Mobile Genetic Elements (MGEs) drive horizontal gene transfer (HGT) and rapid genome evolution in *E. coli*. While MGEs frequently contain clade- or strain-specific accessory sequences that seem ideal for identification, relying on MGE-associated markers introduces two major diagnostic risks:
- **False Positives**: Plasmid or phage transfer into non-target lineages can lead to off-target diagnostic amplification.
- **False Negatives**: Rapid excision or deletion of MGEs under environmental selection can cause marker loss in target strains.

Therefore, demonstrating that diagnostic markers are *not* preferentially enriched in MGEs validates their evolutionary stability and reliability for molecular diagnostics.

** Hypothesis & Null Model
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$)**: Clade-unique FUR markers are distributed randomly across the genome with respect to MGE annotations. The observed number of MGE overlaps is purely a function of overall genome size and baseline MGE coverage.
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$)**: Clade-unique FUR markers are significantly enriched within MGE regions compared to random genomic expectation ($p < 0.05$).

* Methodology

** 1. Marker Isolation & BLAST
Reciprocal BLAST analysis (`venn_prep.sh`) was conducted between *Fur*-generated markers (`markers.fasta`) and *SeqWin*-generated signatures (`signatures.fasta`). 

Using `extract_unique.py`, sequences unique to *Fur* were extracted into `fur_unique.fasta` for each clade. Clades *A* and *B1* yielded no unique sequences and were halted at this stage.

** 2. Reference Coordinate Mapping
For each active clade, the official NCBI reference genome FASTA and GFF3 annotation files were fetched. Unique marker sequences were mapped to reference chromosome coordinates using `make_intervals.py`, producing exact start and end intervals (`fur_only_intervals.txt`).

** 3. Observed Overlap Calculation
Using `annotate_mge.py`, the mapped marker intervals were queried against the reference GFF features. An overlap was counted whenever a marker interval intersected an annotated MGE feature (transposases, integrases, phages, insertion sequences, and plasmids).

** 4. Monte Carlo Permutation Testing
To test for statistical significance:
1. Marker intervals were randomly shuffled across the reference genome $N = 10,000$ times using `shuffle`, maintaining interval length distributions.
2. For each permutation, the count of simulated MGE overlaps was recorded in `shuffle_counts.txt`.
3. The empirical $p$-value was calculated as:
   \[
   p = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbb{I}(\text{Count}_i \ge \text{Observed}) + 1}{N + 1}
   \]

* Detailed Clade-by-Clade Discussion

*** Clade B2
- **Mapped Markers**: 16
- **Observed Overlaps**: 0
- **Null Distribution**: Random shuffles produced a mean of $\approx 1.88$ overlaps per genome, with simulated counts ranging from 0 to 8.
- **Interpretation**: Because 0 overlaps were observed, 100% of randomized configurations produced $\ge 0$ overlaps ($p = 1.0$). Markers for *B2* are completely devoid of mobile elements and located entirely within stable chromosomal backbone regions.

*** Clade D
- **Mapped Markers**: 12
- **Observed Overlaps**: 2
- **Null Distribution**: Random shuffling yielded $\ge 2$ overlaps in 2,060 out of 10,000 permutations ($p = 0.2061$).
- **Interpretation**: While 2 markers fell within MGE-annotated regions, a 20.6% chance of observing $\ge 2$ overlaps under random interval distribution indicates no statistically significant enrichment. The 2 overlaps are attributable to baseline MGE density in the *Clade D* reference genome.

*** Clade E
- **Mapped Markers**: 8
- **Observed Overlaps**: 0
- **Null Distribution**: Random shuffles averaged $\approx 1.82$ overlaps.
- **Interpretation**: Similar to *B2*, all 8 unique markers map to non-mobile genomic regions ($p = 1.0$), reinforcing high diagnostic stability.

*** Clade F
- **Mapped Markers**: 12
- **Observed Overlaps**: 2
- **Null Distribution**: Random shuffling produced $\ge 2$ overlaps in 2,007 out of 10,000 permutations ($p = 0.2008$).
- **Interpretation**: Consistent with *Clade D*, the observed overlaps do not deviate significantly from expected background levels ($p > 0.20$).

* Thesis Conclusions & Recommendations

1. **High Diagnostic Quality of FUR Markers**: None of the tested *E. coli* phylogroups showed significant MGE enrichment ($p \gg 0.05$). This indicates that *Fur* primarily targets stable, clade-specific chromosomal regions rather than unstable mobile elements.
2. **Suitability for Primer Design**: Markers identified in *B2*, *D*, *E*, and *F* represent robust candidates for PCR/qPCR assay development, as they carry low risk of horizontal transfer or deletion.
3. **Investigation of Clades A and B1**: Future work should adjust *Fur* specificity parameters for clades *A* and *B1* to recover diagnostic markers for these closely related sister lineages.
